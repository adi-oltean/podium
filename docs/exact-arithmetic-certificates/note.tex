\begin{abstract}
Guidance and control software relies on numerical optimization and
floating-point synthesis whose executions are difficult to verify directly. We
study the problem of verifying solver \emph{outputs}: given a certificate
produced by an untrusted floating-point solver, we re-verify it in exact rational
arithmetic, so that acceptance constitutes a proof that carries no rounding
error. This note documents the exact-rational certificates in the Podium library
(\texttt{podium.verify}) and applies the method to four properties: abort safety,
via barrier certificates; optimality of supplied convex-program solutions, via
Karush--Kuhn--Tucker conditions for quadratic and second-order cone programs;
closed-loop stability, via Lyapunov ellipsoids from a discrete-time
linear-quadratic regulator; and polynomial set containment, via sum-of-squares
certificates. These certificates are standard; what we give is exact
re-verification, together with their prior-art positioning.
The QP and SOCP diagnostic flags may use tolerances; their certified bounds
require the stated dual-feasibility conditions, with exact stationarity and
exact conic dual feasibility on the SOCP path. The other certificate checks
are tolerance-free. A reference implementation tests representative instances.

For nonconvex QCQPs we specialize the exact rational recovery of a semidefinite
certificate, developed for polynomial optimization by Peyrl and Parrilo and by
Kaltofen et al., to the S-procedure dual. This yields a tolerance-free,
machine-checkable optimality-gap bracket: a rational lower bound from the dual
and a rational upper bound from an exactly feasible rational point. Coincident
accepted endpoints certify global optimality; exact closure requires optimal
rational witnesses. We analyze the local loss in the lower bound as
at most second order in multiplier error at a nonsingular interior optimum, and first
order at a singular endpoint with nonzero limiting slope. Numerical proposal
error and rational-rounding error both contribute to the interior bound
(Theorem~\ref{thm:recovery}). For several constraints the
S-procedure need not be tight. On a two-ball Celis--Dennis--Tapia instance~\cite{celis1985}, its
bound is $\CdtDualLower$, whereas a degree-four rational sum-of-squares
certificate proves $J^\star\ge\CdtSOSLower$. An exactly feasible point gives
$J^\star\le\CdtUpper$; these bounds certify both an upper bound on that
point's suboptimality and a strictly positive gap above the S-procedure value
(Theorem~\ref{thm:multi} and Appendix~\ref{app:cdt}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Software increasingly makes consequential decisions by solving optimization
problems numerically. A spacecraft plans a fuel-optimal approach; a controller
selects a maneuver that must avoid a keep-out zone; an autopilot commits to a
command computed by a convex solver. In each case the solver returns an answer,
but the answer is a floating-point number produced by an opaque computation
rather than a proof that the answer is correct or optimal. Floating-point
arithmetic rounds at every step, and a computation that appears to succeed can
return a value that is subtly wrong. Where a mistake is costly and cannot be
undone after the fact, as in spaceflight, it is desirable for the solver's
answer to carry a \emph{certificate}: a small, separate object that an
independent program can verify exactly, so that correctness rests on the
verifier rather than on the solver. This note constructs such certificates for
guidance and control, and studies in particular bounds on the global optimum
of a keep-out QCQP primitive relevant to trajectory optimization.

A concrete instance motivates the main results. Nonconvex trajectory
optimization for powered landing is commonly
solved by successive convexification, which linearizes the nonconvex constraints
about the current iterate and solves a convex subproblem, repeating until
convergence \cite{elango2025}. The cited convergence result concerns stationary
points of a penalized problem; correspondence to a KKT point of the constrained
discretized problem requires feasibility and the cited regularity assumptions.
Neither stationarity nor a solver-reported cost
supplies a certified bound on global suboptimality. This note supplies such a
bound for a keep-out QCQP: an exact-arithmetic certificate brackets its global
optimum once both a lower certificate and an exactly feasible rational upper
witness have passed their checks. When the bracket closes, that witness is
globally optimal. The certificate requires neither convexity nor trust in the
solver; a whole-trajectory guarantee would additionally require bounds and
feasibility checks for the complete trajectory problem.

The underlying idea is general. A guidance and control stack executes a large
amount of untrusted
numerical code: convex solvers compute commands, semidefinite programs (SDPs)
synthesize safety and stability certificates, and floating-point routines emit
actuator signals. Verifying the \emph{execution} of these components is possible
only in narrow settings --- for active-set quadratic-programming methods on
multiparametric problems, the exact worst-case iteration count can be certified
offline \cite{arnstrom2022} --- and such analyses are specific to one solver and
one problem family, leaving the floating-point behavior of the routines
themselves uncovered. We instead verify the \emph{answer}: an
untrusted floating-point tool proposes a certificate, and a trusted checker
re-establishes the property in exact rational arithmetic, without floating-point
computation of its own. Because the check is a short, deterministic computation,
it can be re-run automatically whenever the code or data change. This matters in
practice: a candidate certificate produced in floating point may satisfy its
defining condition only approximately, so a tolerance-based check can accept a
pair $(\lambda, t)$ whose value $t$ exceeds the true optimum, whereas the exact
rational test accepts a certificate only when it holds exactly.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item An exact-rational verification layer for four standard guidance and
control certificates, barrier abort safety, Karush--Kuhn--Tucker (KKT)
optimality of supplied convex-program solutions, Lyapunov stability, and sum-of-squares
set containment (Section~\ref{sec:family}), each synthesized by an untrusted
solver and re-checked over the rationals $\Q$. Approximate QP solutions
can receive an explicit rational bound under the stated hypotheses; approximate
SOCP solutions receive exact residuals, with a bound only at exact stationarity
and conic-dual feasibility.
\item A specialization of exact rational SDP-certificate recovery to the
S-procedure dual of a nonconvex quadratically constrained quadratic program
(QCQP), giving a tolerance-free machine-checkable optimality-gap bracket for
guidance and control (Section~\ref{sec:bracket}). We demonstrate it on a
keep-out primitive relevant to successive convexification
(Sections~\ref{sec:bracket} and~\ref{sec:impl}); Section~\ref{sec:scvx}
states what a trajectory application would additionally require.
\item A multiplier-error sensitivity analysis of that bracket: the certified lower bound loses
value at most quadratically in multiplier error at a nonsingular
interior maximizer
(Theorem~\ref{thm:recovery}) and at first order at a singular endpoint with
nonzero limiting slope (Theorem~\ref{thm:hard}), with exact acceptance in both. The
analysis in Theorem~\ref{thm:recovery} separates numerical proposal error
from rational-rounding error.
The bracket width bounds the suboptimality of the feasible point and also the
duality gap (Theorem~\ref{thm:multi}); a degree-four certificate proves the
gap strictly positive on the two-ball example (Appendix~\ref{app:cdt}).
\item An open reference implementation whose trusted checker uses no floating
point, with tests that reconstruct representative certificates over $\Q$
(Section~\ref{sec:impl}).
\end{enumerate}

None of the underlying certificates is new. The barrier certificates are due to
Prajna and Jadbabaie \cite{prajna2004} and Ames et al.\ \cite{ames2017}; the
Lyapunov re-verification follows Wang et al.\ \cite{feron2013}; the S-lemma is
due to Yakubovich \cite{yakubovich1971} and its exactness is surveyed by P\'olik
and Terlaky \cite{polik2007}. Most importantly, the recovery of an exact rational
certificate from a floating-point SDP solution, and its use to certify global
lower bounds in polynomial optimization, is due to Peyrl and Parrilo
\cite{peyrl2008}, Kaltofen et al.\ \cite{kaltofen2012}, and Magron and Safey El
Din \cite{magron2018}; Davis and Papp \cite{davis2022} recover exact rational
certificates directly from a numerical dual certificate and prove a convergence
rate, and rigorous bracketing of a relaxation value is provided by verified SDP
\cite{jansson2009,roux2018}. A nonconvex QCQP is the degree-two case of this
machinery, and its exactness and hidden convexity are themselves classical
\cite{bental1996,beck2006,polik2007}. Our contribution is therefore not exact
rational certification, the bracket idea, nor certificate recovery with a rate,
but the specialization of these to the S-procedure dual of a QCQP, in particular
the local multiplier-error bounds and rational-witness conditions of
Section~\ref{sec:bracket}, together with exact checks for a QCQP primitive
relevant to guidance and control.

\section{Exact re-verification of solver output}
\label{sec:discipline}

A \emph{certificate} is a small object that makes a property easy to confirm even
when the property was hard to establish: a Lyapunov matrix for stability, a
multiplier for a bound, a sum-of-squares decomposition for nonnegativity. Our
method separates search from proof. An untrusted floating-point routine searches
for the certificate, typically by solving an SDP or an interior-point problem.
The result is then rounded to nearby rational numbers and checked by an exact
computation over $\Q$.

The single trusted primitive is an exact test for positive semidefiniteness
(PSD), a symmetric $LDL^\top$ elimination over $\Q$ in $O(n^3)$ operations: a
symmetric matrix is PSD if and only if every pivot is nonnegative and each zero
pivot leaves the rest of its row, and hence by symmetry its column, in the Schur
complement zero. The resulting factorization $M = L D L^\top$ with $D \succeq 0$
is itself the nonnegativity witness, since $z^\top M z = \sum_i D_{ii}
(l_i^\top z)^2$. This avoids eigenvalues, which are in general irrational and
admit no exact rational representation, and the leading-minor test of Sylvester,
which certifies strict definiteness only. The trusted checker uses only exact
rational arithmetic, so its conclusions carry no rounding error. For the barrier, Lyapunov, sum-of-squares, and optimality-gap
certificates, acceptance is an exact identity together with the PSD test, with no
tolerance. When the object under test is the output of an approximate solver, as
for the supplied convex-program points below, the residuals are still computed exactly, but
a rigorous bound on suboptimality is reported only when those exact residuals
yield a valid Lagrangian dual point, not merely when they are small.

\section{Four certificate families}
\label{sec:family}

\paragraph{Barrier certificates for abort safety.}
For linearized relative motion with a spherical keep-out zone, an SDP produces a
barrier function whose sublevel set is invariant under the passive, thrust-free
drift. The checker verifies over $\Q$ an S-procedure condition, in which a
nonnegative multiplier certifies that one quadratic is nonnegative on the region
where another is nonnegative. The barrier is expressed on a basis of quantities
conserved by the passive drift, so its Lie derivative --- the rate of change of
the barrier along the drift --- vanishes by construction. Together these
establish forward invariance of the safe set, so that the abort set never
intersects the keep-out zone \cite{prajna2004,ames2017}.

\paragraph{KKT certificates for supplied convex-program solutions.}
The library exposes checks for a quadratic program (QP) and a second-order
cone program (SOCP). Tests exercise representative instances; the guidance
planners do not invoke these checkers. Given a supplied primal-dual point,
the checker computes KKT residuals, stationarity, primal and dual
feasibility, and complementary slackness, exactly. A small stationarity residual
is not by itself a bound on suboptimality: with a rank-deficient objective (a
linear or minimum-fuel program) an approximately stationary point can be
arbitrarily far from optimal. The checker therefore reports a rigorous bound only
when a valid Lagrangian dual point exists, namely when the multiplier is
dual-feasible, the Hessian $P$ is PSD, and the stationarity residual $r$ lies in
the range of $P$. The exact objective-minus-dual-value quantity
$\mu^\top(h - Gx) + \nu^\top(b - Ax) + \tfrac12\, r^\top P^{+} r$ then bounds
$p(x) - p^\star$, the term $\tfrac12\, r^\top P^{+} r$ being the objective drop
from the candidate to the exact minimizer of the Lagrangian, computed by solving
$P z = r$ over the rationals. If $x$ is only feasible within a tolerance,
$p(x)-p^\star$ can be negative; a feasible upper certificate requires exact
primal feasibility. A SOCP objective is linear and so has no curvature
to absorb a residual; a rigorous bound there instead requires exact conic-dual
feasibility. Membership of a slack vector
$s = (s_0, s_{1:}) \in \R \times \R^d$ in the second-order cone is decided without
square roots through the algebraic condition $s_0 \ge 0$ and
$\lVert s_{1:}\rVert^2 \le s_0^2$. The exact conic predicate gates the bound;
diagnostic membership flags also expose a tolerance-based check.
The QP result likewise exposes a tolerance-based \texttt{certified(tol)} flag;
that flag does not replace the exact conditions for a rigorous bound. The QP path
verifies a PSD objective Hessian; the SOCP path has a linear objective.
These conditions support global, rather than merely stationary, bounds.

\paragraph{Lyapunov certificates for closed-loop stability.}
A cost-to-go matrix $P$ from a discrete-time linear-quadratic regulator (LQR) is
re-verified as positive definite, so that its sublevel sets are bounded
ellipsoids, and as satisfying the Lyapunov decrease condition
$P - A_{\mathrm{cl}}^\top P A_{\mathrm{cl}} \succeq 0$, where
$A_{\mathrm{cl}} = A - BK$ is the closed-loop transition matrix under the
state-feedback gain $K$, using the same exact PSD test, in the sense of
\cite{feron2013}.

\paragraph{Sum-of-squares (SOS) certificates for set containment.}
The nonnegativity of a polynomial $p(x)$, for instance that a certified attitude
cone contains a given sublevel set, is verified as an identity
$p(x) = z(x)^\top G\, z(x)$ with $G$ symmetric positive semidefinite, where
$z(x)$ is a vector of monomials and $G$ is the associated Gram matrix. Such a
representation exhibits $p$ as a sum of squares, and hence as nonnegative. The Gram matrix is recovered from an approximate SDP
solution by the rational rounding of Peyrl and Parrilo \cite{peyrl2008}.

\section{Optimality-gap bounds for nonconvex QCQPs}
\label{sec:bracket}

Quadratically constrained quadratic programs arise throughout guidance: keep-out
zones, thrust limits, and
terminal ellipsoids are quadratic, and many convex subproblems inside
successive convexification loops use QP or second-order-cone formulations.
When the constraints are nonconvex the problem is NP-hard in general, and a
solver can return a feasible point but not, on its own, a proof that no better
point exists. We construct such a proof in exact arithmetic: a lower bound $t$
and an upper bound $u$ with $t \le J^\star \le u$, both rational and both checked
without floating point, so that $u - t$ is a certified upper bound on
suboptimality and the
case $t = u$ certifies a global optimum (Figure~\ref{fig:bracket}).

Consider the QCQP
\begin{equation}
J^\star = \inf_{x \in \R^n}\Big\{ f_0(x) : f_k(x) \ge 0,\ k = 1, \dots, m \Big\},
\qquad f_j(x) = x^\top P_j x + q_j^\top x + r_j,
\label{eq:qcqp}
\end{equation}
with symmetric $P_j\in\Q^{n\times n}$, $q_j\in\Q^n$, and $r_j\in\Q$ for
$j=0,\ldots,m$. Submitted multipliers, lower bounds, and upper witnesses are
rational; the optimization variables and dual optima need not be. A keep-out
constraint $\lVert x - c \rVert \ge R$ renders
the feasible set nonconvex. Throughout we use the instance
$\min \lVert x \rVert^2$ subject to $\lVert x - (3,0) \rVert \ge 5$, whose origin
lies inside the excluded ball; its global optimum is $J^\star = 4$, attained at
$(-2, 0)$ with multiplier $\lambda^\star = 2/5$. Table~\ref{tab:results} lists
this and the other cases considered below.

\subsection{The bracket and its soundness}

The lower bound is a Lagrangian relaxation, certified exactly. For multipliers
$\lambda \in \R^m_{\ge 0}$ the Lagrangian
$L(x, \lambda) = f_0(x) - \sum_k \lambda_k f_k(x)$ is quadratic in $x$, and with
the homogenized coordinate $z = [x; 1] \in \R^{n+1}$ the shifted form
$L(x, \lambda) - t$ equals $z^\top M(\lambda, t) z$, where
\begin{equation}
M(\lambda, t) = \begin{bmatrix}
P_0 - \sum_k \lambda_k P_k & \tfrac12(q_0 - \sum_k \lambda_k q_k) \\[2pt]
\tfrac12(q_0 - \sum_k \lambda_k q_k)^\top & r_0 - \sum_k \lambda_k r_k - t
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:M}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Lower bound]\label{thm:sound}
If $\lambda \ge 0$ componentwise and $M(\lambda, t) \succeq 0$, then
$t \le J^\star$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For any $x$, writing $z = [x; 1]$, expansion gives
$z^\top M(\lambda, t) z = f_0(x) - \sum_k \lambda_k f_k(x) - t$. Positive
semidefiniteness of $M$ makes this nonnegative for all $x$. At a feasible $x$,
each $f_k(x) \ge 0$ and each $\lambda_k \ge 0$, so
$\sum_k \lambda_k f_k(x) \ge 0$ and hence $f_0(x) \ge t$. Taking the infimum over
feasible $x$ gives $t \le J^\star$.
\end{proof}

The argument is Lagrangian weak duality and uses no convexity, constraint
qualification, or boundedness. Conversely, on the primal side, any point
$\bar x$ with $f_k(\bar x) \ge 0$ for all $k$ gives $J^\star \le f_0(\bar x)$. The
feasibility of $\bar x$ must be checked exactly: a point that is feasible only
within a solver tolerance may have objective value below $J^\star$, which would
place the upper bound below the true optimum. The checker therefore evaluates
$f_k(\bar x) \ge 0$ over the rationals. By elementary gap closure, if a certified
lower bound $t$ equals a certified upper bound $u = f_0(\bar x)$ then
$J^\star = t = u$, an exact certificate of the global optimum. The two bounds are
the rational S-procedure lower certificate and a rational feasible point,
standard ingredients of verified global optimization; the content of this section
is their specialization to the QCQP dual and the multiplier-error analysis below.

\subsection{Recovery of an exact bound from an approximate dual}

Fix $m=1$ and write $A(\lambda)=P_0-\lambda P_1$,
$b(\lambda)=q_0-\lambda q_1$, and $c(\lambda)=r_0-\lambda r_1$.
Define the extended-real dual function
$g(\lambda)=\inf_{x\in\R^n}(f_0(x)-\lambda f_1(x))$ for all real $\lambda$;
only $\lambda\ge0$ is dual-feasible. The set
$U=\{\lambda:A(\lambda)\succ0\}$ is the domain of the inverse formula below,
not the full domain of $g$. In fact,
\[
g(\lambda)=\begin{cases}
c(\lambda)-\tfrac14 b(\lambda)^\top A(\lambda)^\dagger b(\lambda),
 & A(\lambda)\succeq0,\ b(\lambda)\in\operatorname{range}A(\lambda),\\
-\infty, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Here $\dagger$ denotes the Moore--Penrose inverse. At a rational multiplier
with finite $g$, one can instead solve $Aw=b$ over $\Q$ and evaluate
$g=c-b^\top w/4$; this does not require computing a pseudoinverse.
We suppress the arguments of $b$ and $c$ within individual formulas.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:schur}
For $\lambda\in U$, $g(\lambda)=c-\tfrac14 b^\top A(\lambda)^{-1}b$, and
$M(\lambda,t)\succeq0$ if and only if $t\le g(\lambda)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On $U$ the quadratic $f_0-\lambda f_1=x^\top Ax+b^\top x+c$ is strictly
convex, with unique minimizer $x(\lambda)=-A^{-1}b/2$ and minimum value
$c-b^\top A^{-1}b/4$. The Schur complement of $A$ in $M(\lambda,t)$ is
$c-t-b^\top A^{-1}b/4$, so $M(\lambda,t)\succeq0$ exactly when this scalar
is nonnegative.
\end{proof}

On $U$, Cramer's rule makes $g$ a rational function of $\lambda$ with rational
coefficients. Thus a rational $\lambda\in U\cap\R_{\ge0}$ yields a rational
lower bound that the exact PSD test certifies. For a single constraint with a
strictly feasible point and finite $J^\star$, the S-lemma gives strong duality,
$J^\star=\max_{\lambda\ge0}g(\lambda)=g(\lambda^\star)$
\cite{yakubovich1971}.

\begin{theorem}[Nonsingular recovery]\label{thm:recovery}
Suppose $A(\lambda^\star)\succ0$ and $\lambda^\star>0$ maximizes the dual.
Then $g(\lambda^\star)=J^\star$ and there is a neighborhood of $\lambda^\star$
in which
\[
0\le J^\star-g(\lambda)\le C|\lambda-\lambda^\star|^2.
\]
Rounding the exact multiplier to a rational $\widehat\lambda_D$ of denominator
at most $D$ with error at most $1/D$ gives an exactly certified bound with
loss $O(D^{-2})$ for sufficiently large $D$.
If instead the numerical proposal $\widetilde\lambda_D$ has error
$\eta_D=|\widetilde\lambda_D-\lambda^\star|$, rounding with error at most $1/D$
gives
\[
J^\star-g(\widehat\lambda_D)=O\bigl((\eta_D+D^{-1})^2\bigr),
\]
provided the total error is small enough to remain in that neighborhood.
In particular, $\eta_D=O(D^{-1})$ or exact recovery is sufficient for the
rate $O(D^{-2})$; a denominator cap alone gives no such guarantee.
If $\lambda^\star$ is rational, so are $J^\star$ and the corresponding
optimizer, and their certificates close the bracket exactly.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On $U$, $g$ is smooth and concave, with unique Lagrangian minimizer
$x(\lambda)=-A(\lambda)^{-1}b(\lambda)/2$. Differentiating the minimum gives
$g'(\lambda)=-f_1(x(\lambda))$. Since $\lambda^\star>0$,
$g'(\lambda^\star)=0$, so $x(\lambda^\star)$ is feasible with $f_1=0$ and
$f_0(x(\lambda^\star))=g(\lambda^\star)=J^\star$ by weak duality.
Choose a closed neighborhood inside $U\cap\R_{>0}$; boundedness of $g''$ and
Taylor's theorem give the quadratic bound. The triangle inequality gives
$|\widehat\lambda_D-\lambda^\star|\le\eta_D+1/D$. In this neighborhood
Lemma~\ref{lem:schur} certifies the rounded bound exactly. If $\lambda^\star$
is rational, the rational inverse formula gives both $x(\lambda^\star)$ and
$g(\lambda^\star)$ over $\Q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
With several constraints and a positive definite Lagrangian Hessian at a dual
maximizer, $\partial g/\partial\lambda_k=-f_k(x(\lambda))$.
For $\lambda_k^\star>0$ this derivative is zero. At $\lambda_k^\star=0$ it is
nonpositive; it is strictly negative for a strictly slack constraint, but can
be zero for a weakly active constraint. A zero multiplier does not by itself
imply strict slackness.
\end{remark}

For the running instance,
\[
4-g(\lambda)=\frac{25(\lambda-2/5)^2}{1-\lambda},\qquad 0\le\lambda<1.
\]
The reference implementation rounds the fixed floating-point proposal
$\widetilde\lambda=0.4003$ to $\lambda^\star=2/5$ at denominator cap $100$,
recovering $t=4$ exactly. At cap $10000$ it instead returns
$\widehat\lambda=\RoundedMultiplier$ with positive loss $\RoundingLoss$.
Increasing the cap for a fixed inaccurate proposal need not improve the bound;
eventually the rounded value equals that floating-point number's exact binary
rational representation, leaving a nonzero error floor.

\subsection{The singular case}

\begin{theorem}[Singular recovery]\label{thm:hard}
Suppose $J^\star$ is finite and a dual maximizer $\lambda^\star\ge0$ satisfies
$g(\lambda^\star)=J^\star$, with $A^\star=A(\lambda^\star)\succeq0$ singular
and $b^\star=b(\lambda^\star)\in\operatorname{range}A^\star$.
For some $\sigma\in\{-1,1\}$ and $\epsilon>0$, suppose
$\lambda^\star+\sigma\delta\in U\cap\R_{\ge0}$ for $0<\delta<\epsilon$,
the finite limit
\[
\bar x=\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}
-\tfrac12 A(\lambda^\star+\sigma\delta)^{-1}
b(\lambda^\star+\sigma\delta)
\]
exists, and $f_1(\bar x)\ne0$. Then:
(a) a rational $\lambda^\star$ yields a rational exact lower certificate
$(\lambda^\star,J^\star)$, accepted even though its matrix is singular;
(b) $\sigma f_1(\bar x)>0$ and
\[
J^\star-g(\lambda^\star+\sigma\delta)
=|f_1(\bar x)|\,\delta+o(\delta),
\]
so admissible rational multipliers approaching the endpoint yield bounds whose
loss is first-order in multiplier error;
(c) exact rational bracket closure additionally requires a rational feasible
optimizer, and is impossible if $J^\star$ is irrational.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(a) At a rational endpoint solve $A^\star w=b^\star$ over $\Q$. Then
$J^\star=c(\lambda^\star)-(b^\star)^\top w/4$ is rational and
\[
L(x,\lambda^\star)-J^\star
=(x+w/2)^\top A^\star(x+w/2).
\]
This identity gives $M(\lambda^\star,J^\star)\succeq0$. The exact
$LDL^\top$ test accepts a zero pivot precisely when the remaining pivot row
vanishes, and thus accepts singular PSD matrices.
(b) Taking limits in the normal equations gives
$A^\star\bar x=-b^\star/2$, so $\bar x$ minimizes the endpoint Lagrangian.
Consequently $g(\lambda^\star+\sigma\delta)\to g(\lambda^\star)=J^\star$.
Along the positive definite branch the derivative with respect to $\delta$ is
$-\sigma f_1(x(\lambda^\star+\sigma\delta))$, which tends to
$-\sigma f_1(\bar x)$. A first-order expansion at the endpoint proves the
displayed loss formula; maximality and the nonzero hypothesis force
$\sigma f_1(\bar x)>0$.
(c) A closed rational bracket supplies a rational feasible optimizer with
objective equal to its rational lower endpoint, hence a rational $J^\star$.
Conversely, an optimal rational lower certificate and a rational feasible
optimizer close the bracket.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The nullspace component]
Let $N=\ker A^\star$ be invariant under $P_1$. The adjacent positive definite
interval makes $P_1|_N$ invertible, and the normal equations on $N$ and
$N^\perp$ give
\begin{equation}
\bar x=-\tfrac12(A^\star)^\dagger b^\star
       -\tfrac12(P_1|_N)^{-1}\Pi_N q_1.
\label{eq:null-limit}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\Pi_N b^\star=0$, while the $N$ blocks of $A$ and $b$ are
$-\sigma\delta P_1|_N$ and $-\sigma\delta\Pi_N q_1$. The second term vanishes
if $\Pi_N q_1=0$, as in the centered trust-region case $P_1=-I$, $q_1=0$;
$P_1=-I$ alone does not suffice. For a centered ball, $f_1(\bar x)>0$ describes
the classical hard case \cite{moresorensen1983}.
\end{remark}

The theorem describes local sensitivity to multiplier error and preserves the
soundness of supplied singular certificates. The implementation's instance
$\min -x_1^2+2x_2^2+2x_2$ subject to $1-\lVert x\rVert^2\ge0$ has
$A(\lambda)=\operatorname{diag}(-1+\lambda,2+\lambda)$, singular at
$\lambda^\star=1$, with $J^\star=-4/3$. For $\lambda=1+\delta$,
$J^\star-g(\lambda)=\delta[1-1/(3(3+\delta))]$, with limiting slope $8/9$.
The exact certificate $(1,-4/3)$ is accepted, but the implementation's
positive-definite-only recovery routine does not evaluate that endpoint.
Its primal optimizers $(\pm2\sqrt2/3,-1/3)$ are irrational, so a rational
upper witness cannot close this bracket even though its optimal lower
certificate is rational. Applying the change of variable $x_1\mapsto x_1-a$
to both objective and constraint, with rational $a$, gives $\bar x=(a,-1/3)$, whereas the pseudoinverse term alone gives
$(0,-1/3)$, illustrating the extra term in \eqref{eq:null-limit}.

\subsection{Several constraints}

Define the S-procedure dual value
\[
d^\star=\sup\{t:\lambda\in\R^m_{\ge0},\ t\in\R,\ M(\lambda,t)\succeq0\}.
\]
It is distinct from the Shor \emph{primal} relaxation value $v_{\rm Shor}$,
obtained by replacing $xx^\top$ by $X$ and imposing
$\left[\begin{smallmatrix}X&x\\x^\top&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\succeq0$.
SDP weak duality gives $d^\star\le v_{\rm Shor}$, and relaxation gives
$v_{\rm Shor}\le J^\star$; identifying the first
two values requires SDP strong duality, for example a suitable Slater condition
with finite relaxation value. We do not impose such regularity in the next
result.

\begin{theorem}[Certified bounds on suboptimality and duality gap]\label{thm:multi}
For every $m$, a rational certificate $\lambda\ge0$, $M(\lambda,t)\succeq0$
and a rational exactly feasible point $\bar x$, with $u=f_0(\bar x)$, give
\[
t\le d^\star\le J^\star\le u.
\]
In particular,
\[
0\le u-J^\star\le u-t,\qquad 0\le J^\star-d^\star\le u-t.
\]
Thus $[t,u]$ encloses the optimum, and its width bounds both the suboptimality
of $\bar x$ and the S-procedure duality gap. If $t=u$, these certificates
establish global optimality. For $m\ge2$, $d^\star<J^\star$ is possible.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The supplied multiplier and bound are feasible for the supremum defining
$d^\star$, so $t\le d^\star$. Theorem~\ref{thm:sound} holds for every
nonnegative real multiplier; taking the supremum gives $d^\star\le J^\star$.
Exact feasibility gives $J^\star\le u$. These finite endpoints imply both
displayed gap bounds by subtraction. If the endpoints coincide, all
intermediate quantities coincide. The two-ball example below proves that
strict S-procedure gaps can occur with two constraints.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
For $m=1$ with a strictly feasible point and finite $J^\star$, the S-lemma gives
$d^\star=J^\star$. This equality over $\R$ does not imply that optimal lower
certificates or feasible optimizers exist over $\Q$. For instance,
$\min(x-2)^2$ subject to $2-x^2\ge0$ has irrational optimum $6-4\sqrt2$,
so no rational bracket closes exactly despite a nonsingular dual optimum.
For several constraints, the optimal \emph{real} SOS bounds in the
moment/SOS hierarchy converge to $J^\star$ under an Archimedean assumption
\cite{lasserre2001}. Turning a numerical candidate into an exact rational lower
certificate additionally requires rational Gram matrices, exact coefficient
identities, and PSD verification. Real convergence alone does not guarantee
that arbitrary rounding produces such certificates.
\end{remark}

For the two-ball Celis--Dennis--Tapia instance~\cite{celis1985}
\[
\min 2x_1x_2\quad\text{subject to}\quad
\lVert x-(1,0)\rVert\le2,\quad\lVert x+(1,0)\rVert\le2,
\]
the S-procedure dual has value $d^\star=\CdtDualLower$. Its rational upper
witness gives the exact enclosure $[\CdtDualLower,\CdtUpper]$.
Appendix~\ref{app:cdt} supplies a degree-four rational SOS identity proving
$J^\star\ge\CdtSOSLower$. Hence
\[
J^\star\in[\CdtSOSLower,\CdtUpper],\qquad
0\le u-J^\star\le\CdtImprovedWidth,
\]
and
\[
\CdtGapLower\le J^\star-d^\star\le\CdtGapUpper.
\]
The positive lower endpoint proves a strict S-procedure duality gap exactly.
The higher-degree certificate improves the objective enclosure without changing
$d^\star$, which refers only to the original quadratic relaxation.

On the two-ball instance of Appendix~\ref{app:cdt}, the Shor primal
attains $-3$ at $x=0$ and $X=\tfrac32\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]$.
Thus the positive certified QCQP relaxation gap there coexists with zero
SDP primal--dual gap. In the note's revision artifacts,
\texttt{revision-2026-09-08/verify\_revision.py} computes this Shor witness's
PSD verdict, constraint slacks and objective. It assembles the dual minorant
matrix from the quadratic data and supplied multipliers, checks its PSD
verdict and complementary inner product, and replays the exact degree-four
witness check. Its singular-endpoint controls retain exact minimizer
sequences and derive limiting points from both diagonal and rationally
rotated problem data. These checks are separate from the library tests.
The earlier \texttt{revision-2026-09-06/run\_checks.py} invokes the
manuscript gates from the paper repository and runs library tests; those
gates are outside the revision artifact folder. That runner does not check
the Shor witness.

\subsection{Relation to successive convexification}
\label{sec:scvx}

Nonconvex trajectory optimization in guidance is commonly addressed by successive
convexification and sequential convex programming, which repeatedly convexify
about the current iterate and solve convex subproblems \cite{elango2025}.
Their convergence statements depend on the method's assumptions: a stationary
point need not be locally optimal, and feasibility of the original trajectory
problem must be established. A returned iterate is therefore a proposal for
an upper witness. To use it here, a rational point $\bar x$ must satisfy every
constraint of the original rational QCQP exactly; rational rounding can destroy
feasibility and must be checked.

For that same QCQP, an accepted S-procedure certificate gives a lower bound
$t\le J^\star$, so $u-t$ bounds the global suboptimality of the accepted point.
A trajectory-wide claim requires an objective and constraints describing the
entire trajectory problem, including a justified treatment of any
nonquadratic or continuous-time conditions. The demonstrated application here
is the finite-dimensional keep-out QCQP primitive. Certification of a convex
subproblem or of that primitive alone does not establish a global bound for an
entire nonconvex trajectory optimization problem.

\section{Related work}

Exact and certified certificates for global polynomial optimization are an
established subject. The moment/SOS hierarchy of Lasserre \cite{lasserre2001} and
Parrilo \cite{parrilo2003} gives convergent real lower bounds under its regularity assumptions, Henrion and
Lasserre \cite{henrion2005} extract optimizers and detect global optimality, and
Nie, Demmel, and Sturmfels \cite{nie2006} give SOS exactness over the gradient
ideal. Exact rational certificates recovered from a numerical SDP are due to
Peyrl and Parrilo \cite{peyrl2008}, Kaltofen et al.\ \cite{kaltofen2012}, and
Magron and Safey El Din \cite{magron2018}; closest to our rate statement, Davis
and Papp \cite{davis2022} construct exact rational certificates from numerical
dual certificates and prove a convergence rate. Rigorous, though not exact,
bracketing of the relaxation value by verified SDP is due to Jansson
\cite{jansson2009} and Roux et al.\ \cite{roux2018}, and an exact SDP duality
theory to Ramana \cite{ramana1997}. A nonconvex QCQP is the degree-two instance
of this machinery; its duality, exactness, and hidden convexity are classical
\cite{yakubovich1971,shor1987,polik2007,bental1996,beck2006,jeyakumar2014}, as is
the trust-region hard case \cite{moresorensen1983,adachi2017}.

Against this background our contribution is narrow. We instantiate the exact
rational recovery of Peyrl and Parrilo and of Davis and Papp on the S-procedure
dual matrix $M(\lambda,t)$ of a QCQP; we record the local sensitivity of the
certified lower bound to multiplier error, at most second-order at a
nonsingular interior maximizer (Theorem~\ref{thm:recovery}), and first-order
at a singular endpoint with an adjacent positive-definite branch and nonzero
limiting slope (Theorem~\ref{thm:hard}). This is the standard second-order-versus-first-order
local behavior at stationary interior and nonstationary boundary maximizers,
stated for the QCQP dual; we do not claim it as new. The weak-duality bracket and its
closure are likewise standard. The contribution is the integration: an
exact-rational implementation of the QCQP primitive and its certificate checks,
with the conditions for a guidance application stated in
Section~\ref{sec:scvx}. Barrier and control-barrier
certificates \cite{prajna2004,ames2017}, credible autocoding \cite{feron2013},
and the verification of convex-optimization solvers for model-predictive control
\cite{cohen2020} are complementary in verifying control software: the last
verifies the solver algorithm, whereas we verify its output. Lossless
convexification \cite{acikmese2011} and successive convexification
\cite{elango2025} address the feasibility and tightness of convex reformulations;
applying the bounds here to a resulting trajectory would additionally require
certificates for the complete original problem (Section~\ref{sec:scvx}).

\section{Limitations and open problems}
\label{sec:limits}

Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:hard}, at a rational singular multiplier,
the dual can be evaluated through a consistent rational solve and PSD verification. The
reference recovery routine implements neither that endpoint evaluation nor
the search for a singular boundary certificate from approximate data.
It uses a positive-definite solve; a supplied singular pair can still pass the
PSD verifier. Approaching from the inadmissible side yields no certificate. Even with an optimal lower
certificate, rational bracket closure can be impossible because the optimizer
or optimum is irrational. The local sensitivity results do not remove numerical
proposal error or guarantee convergence as a denominator cap increases.

The degree-four certificate in Appendix~\ref{app:cdt} resolves strictness of the
gap for that instance; it does not compute the exact irrational optimum by
rational gap closure or provide a general finite-level recovery method.
The implementation evidence concerns individual primitives, not an entire
trajectory optimization problem. The partial Lean coverage is stated in
Section~\ref{sec:impl}.

The $LDL^\top$ PSD test takes $O(n^3)$ rational arithmetic operations; this is
not a bit-complexity bound. Intermediate numerators and denominators can grow
under exact elimination. The examples establish feasibility at their stated
sizes, not scalability to arbitrary guidance problems.

\section{Conclusion}

We have described exact rational verification of certificates proposed by
untrusted guidance and control computations, with checkers for barrier safety,
KKT conditions and qualified optimality bounds, Lyapunov stability, and SOS
nonnegativity. For a nonconvex QCQP, an accepted lower certificate and an
exactly feasible rational upper witness enclose the global optimum. The local
loss of the lower bound is at most quadratic in multiplier error at a
nonsingular interior maximizer and linear at a singular endpoint with nonzero
limiting slope. Approximate proposals contribute their own error.
A degree-four rational SOS certificate tightens the two-ball example's enclosure
and proves its S-procedure duality gap positive. These constructions are
realized in an open reference implementation and accompanying exact witnesses.

Write the coordinates as $(x,y)$ and the constraints as
$g_\pm(x,y)=3-x^2-y^2\pm2x\ge0$. For nonnegative constant multipliers
$\lambda_+,\lambda_-$, set $s=\lambda_++\lambda_-$ and
$h=\lambda_--\lambda_+$. The Lagrangian quadratic coefficient matrix is
$\left[\begin{smallmatrix}s&1\\1&s\end{smallmatrix}\right]$, so finite dual
values require $s\ge1$. For $s>1$ the dual value is
\[
-3s-\frac{h^2s}{s^2-1}\le-3s<-3.
\]
At $s=1$, the range condition forces $h=0$, yielding $g=-3$.
Thus $(\lambda_+,\lambda_-)=(1/2,1/2)$ certifies the optimal S-procedure
lower bound $d^\star=\CdtDualLower$.

The rational matrices below satisfy
\begin{equation}
\tfrac32+2xy
=v_2^\top G_0v_2+g_+v_1^\top G_+v_1+g_-v_1^\top G_-v_1.
\label{eq:cdt-sos}
\end{equation}
